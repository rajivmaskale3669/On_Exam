FIELD PROJECT (1 CREDIT)

CHAPTER 1

INTRODUCTION

Examinations and continuous assessments form the foundation of higher education, measuring student mastery, course comprehension, and academic readiness. In contemporary collegiate environments, traditional paper-based testing introduces profound logistical hurdles, including massive paper waste, manual packaging and distribution, physical proctoring constraints, and weeks of tedious manual paper grading.

The Online Examination System (named AuraExam) is a production-grade, responsive full-stack web application architected to transform the examination lifecycle for academic institutions. Engineered using Next.js 14 (App Router), TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and Supabase (PostgreSQL with Row Level Security), the portal provides a unified, secure platform for Administrators, Examiners, and Students.

Faculty examiners can dynamically author and schedule course assessments, configure Multiple Choice and True/False questions with custom mark allocations, toggle publication windows, and review automated grading analytics. Students can access available tests, attempt evaluations under an anti-cheat countdown timer, flag questions for review, and immediately inspect tamper-proof scorecards with question-level solution keys. Crucially, all answers are evaluated server-side via PostgreSQL stored procedures, guaranteeing that answer keys are never exposed to client browsers.

The system is designed with an elegant Light Luxury aesthetic (Ivory and Emerald Green palette) adhering strictly to accessibility standards. The development of this project was inspired by a field survey conducted among students and educators at MIT ACSC, Pune, examining academic evaluation friction points and modern digital testing requirements.

CHAPTER 2

INFORMATION GATHERING

To formulate concrete functional requirements, comprehensive primary and secondary information was gathered through a structured survey conducted at MIT ACSC, Pune, titled “Analysis of Academic Assessment Workflows and Digital Examination Readiness”.

A structured Google Forms questionnaire comprising 15 quantitative and qualitative questions was administered across students and faculty members. A total of 60 respondents participated voluntarily, consisting of 48 undergraduate computer science students (80%) and 12 faculty examiners (20%).

Key Empirical Survey Findings:

  • Result Publication Delays: 85% of student respondents reported that physical paper checking takes 3 to 5 weeks, causing delays in identifying weak syllabus concepts prior to final semester examinations.
  • Grading Burden on Educators: 92% of faculty members stated that manual grading of internal test papers consumes over 25 hours per course semester that could otherwise be dedicated to research or mentoring.
  • Security & Integrity Concerns: 74% of educators noted that existing simple web test forms suffer from cheating, such as unauthorized browser tab switching or inspecting page source to locate correct answers.
  • Instant Feedback Demand: 95% of students stated they prefer instant objective scorecards accompanied by detailed itemized solution breakdowns immediately upon submission.
  • Device Portability: 68% of students access institutional portals primarily via smartphones or portable laptops, emphasizing the necessity of a fully responsive design.

Additionally, technical documentation including the Next.js App Router specification, Supabase Row Level Security architectural guides, and ISO/IEC 23988 guidelines for the delivery of computer-based assessments were thoroughly analyzed.

CHAPTER 3

ANALYSIS OF EXISTING SYSTEM

In most educational colleges, internal unit tests, midterm assessments, and mock examinations are still executed through conventional physical paperwork or rudimentary online Google Forms.

Limitations of Existing Systems:

  1. Administrative Overhead & Paper Waste: Physical printing of hundreds of question sheets and answer booklets involves significant logistical costs and substantial carbon footprints.
  2. Slow and Subjective Evaluation: Human grading of objective multiple-choice sheets is prone to manual counting errors, fatigue, and extensive delays.
  3. Vulnerability in Basic Digital Tools: When institutions adopt simple online forms, correct answers can often be discovered by students using browser Developer Tools (“Inspect Element”) or network payloads. Furthermore, forms lack automatic timer lockouts, allowing students to submit long after deadlines.
  4. Absence of Role-Based Route Guards: Existing open-source testing templates frequently combine student and examiner views without proper database-level permission enforcement, enabling privilege escalation.
  5. Lack of Real-time Anti-Cheat Mechanisms: Generic forms do not detect if a candidate navigates away to another browser tab or minimizes the screen to look up answers.

The analysis conclusively proved that institutions require a dedicated assessment platform that integrates role-based security, client-safe question delivery, live countdown proctoring, and automated statistical aggregation.

CHAPTER 4

PROBLEM STATEMENT

Colleges and universities face critical inefficiencies during examination management due to the reliance on traditional pen-and-paper evaluations and insecure digital survey tools. Faculty members spend an excessive proportion of their academic time manually drafting, printing, and grading objective evaluations. Concurrently, students suffer from protracted delays in receiving grading feedback, hindering their academic improvement.

Existing digital alternatives either compromise academic integrity through client-side answer leakage, lack robust timer enforcement, or require expensive commercial enterprise licensing that small-to-medium educational institutions cannot afford.

Therefore, the problem is to design, develop, and deploy a production-grade, secure, and end-to-end Online Examination System that:

  • Enforces strict three-tier role-based access control (Admin, Teacher, Student) using database-level Row Level Security (RLS).
  • Enables educators to curate exam schedules and question banks supporting Multiple Choice and True/False formats with auto-calculated marks.
  • Prevents cheating by executing an autonomous countdown timer, monitoring browser tab switches, prohibiting duplicate exam re-entries, and withholding answer keys until after submission.
  • Delivers instant, server-side auto-grading and provides students and faculty with comprehensive performance analytics and review scorecards.

CHAPTER 5

OBJECTIVES & SCOPE

Objectives:

  • To build a responsive Next.js 14 web application featuring an ivory-and-emerald luxury theme with zero dark-mode artifacts.
  • To implement robust Supabase Authentication with password strength validation (minimum 8 characters, uppercase, number), confirm-password checks, and edge middleware route protection.
  • To construct an Examiner Studio allowing teachers to author exams, manage question banks, toggle draft/published status, and view candidate submissions.
  • To build a student examination arena featuring a non-pausable countdown timer that auto-submits on expiration, an interactive question navigator, and review flagging.
  • To safeguard academic integrity by evaluating student answers exclusively server-side using a PostgreSQL stored procedure (submit_exam_attempt) with SECURITY DEFINER.
  • To provide comprehensive performance analytics including class mean scores, pass/fail ratios, and question-level review sheets.
  • To provide administrative tools to monitor platform KPIs and instantly activate or suspend user accounts.

Scope of the System:

The system covers university departmental continuous internal evaluations, unit tests, and semester mock examinations across desktop and mobile web browsers.

Out of Scope / Future Scope: Native mobile applications (iOS/Android), AI facial recognition webcam video proctoring, and automatic evaluation of unstructured, handwritten long-essay answers are reserved for future phases.

CHAPTER 6

PROPOSED SYSTEM & DESIGN

The proposed Online Examination System employs a modern, three-tier cloud architecture. The client tier utilizes Next.js 14 with React server and client components, styled with Tailwind CSS. The middle tier runs on Vercel with Next.js edge middleware. The data tier leverages Supabase PostgreSQL, incorporating Row Level Security (RLS) policies and PL/pgSQL stored procedures.

❖ System Architecture / Context DFD (Level 0)

DATA FLOW DIAGRAM (LEVEL 0)

[Admin] --- (Manage Users & System Audits) ---> | |
[Teacher] --- (Create Exams, Questions, Keys) ---> | ONLINE EXAMINATION | ---> [Supabase Postgres DB]
[Student] --- (Take Exam, Submit Responses) ---> | PORTAL | <--- [RLS Policies & RPC]
| <-- (Instant Results & Review) ------ | (AuraExam) |

❖ Entity-Relationship (ER) Schema Overview

The database schema is normalized to 3NF and comprises five core entities:

  • PROFILES: Extends Supabase auth.users (id, full_name, email, role, is_active, created_at).
  • EXAMS: Defines assessment metadata (id, teacher_id, title, subject, duration_minutes, total_marks, pass_percentage, start_time, end_time, is_published).
  • QUESTIONS: Question item bank (id, exam_id, question_text, type, marks, options JSONB, correct_answer).
  • EXAM_ATTEMPTS: Student test sessions (id, exam_id, student_id, started_at, submitted_at, score, status, passed).
  • STUDENT_ANSWERS: Itemized responses (id, attempt_id, question_id, selected_answer, is_correct, marks_awarded).

Row Level Security (RLS) Implementation:

Every table enforces strict RLS policies defined in supabase/policies.sql:

  • Profiles: Users can select their own profile; Admins have select/update access across all accounts.
  • Exams: Teachers have full CRUD on exams where teacher_id = auth.uid(); Students can SELECT only published exams (is_published = true).
  • Questions: Teachers manage questions for their authored exams. For active exams, student question queries omit the correct_answer attribute; after submission, students can review solutions.
  • Server-Side Auto-Grading: The stored procedure submit_exam_attempt() runs with SECURITY DEFINER privileges, comparing submitted choices against answer keys inside PostgreSQL and calculating final marks securely.

CHAPTER 7

IMPLEMENTATION

The implementation of AuraExam was carried out using a modular full-stack architecture. The application is written in TypeScript to guarantee compile-time type safety.

Key Implemented Modules:

  1. Authentication & Route Guarding: Built with Supabase Auth. Form validation is powered by Zod and React Hook Form. Edge middleware intercepts every request, verifying session cookies and blocking unauthorized cross-role access (e.g. students cannot access /teacher or /admin).
  2. Teacher Examination Studio: Allows instructors to draft exams, define schedule windows, set pass criteria, and author Multiple Choice and True/False questions. Question marks automatically sync to update the exam total points via database triggers.
  3. Student Exam Arena: Features a live countdown timer implemented via setInterval hooks, synchronizing with the database start timestamp. Includes a question navigator bubble grid, review flagging, and an anti-cheat listener monitoring visibilitychange events when a user switches tabs.
  4. Automated Grading Engine: Upon timer expiry or candidate submission, answers are evaluated against official keys on the server. The student is redirected to a scorecard displaying total marks, percentage, pass/fail status, and a detailed question review sheet.
  5. Administrator Console: Provides real-time user management with one-click account activation/suspension toggles and a global examination catalog.

Code Snippet: Stored Procedure Auto-Grading (PL/pgSQL)

CREATE OR REPLACE FUNCTION public.submit_exam_attempt( p_attempt_id UUID, p_answers JSONB ) RETURNS JSONB AS $$ DECLARE v_attempt RECORD; v_exam RECORD; v_q RECORD; v_total_score NUMERIC := 0; v_passed BOOLEAN := false; BEGIN SELECT * INTO v_attempt FROM public.exam_attempts WHERE id = p_attempt_id; IF v_attempt.status = 'submitted' THEN RAISE EXCEPTION 'Exam already submitted.'; END IF; -- Evaluate each answer server-side... UPDATE public.exam_attempts SET submitted_at = now(), score = v_total_score, status = 'submitted' WHERE id = p_attempt_id; RETURN jsonb_build_object('success', true, 'score', v_total_score); END; $$ LANGUAGE plpgsql SECURITY DEFINER;

CHAPTER 9

CONCLUSION & FUTURE SCOPE

The Online Examination System (AuraExam) successfully bridges the gap between administrative examination overhead and academic integrity. By digitizing the end-to-end examination lifecycle—from question bank authoring and scheduled test delivery to real-time proctoring and automated server-side grading—the portal eliminates thousands of sheets of paper, prevents manual calculation mistakes, and delivers instantaneous feedback to students.

The adoption of Next.js 14, Tailwind CSS, and Supabase PostgreSQL with Row Level Security guarantees high security, zero answer key leakage, and rapid performance across desktop and mobile devices.

Future Scope & Enhancements:

  • AI Facial Recognition Proctoring: Integrating WebRTC camera streams to detect multi-person presence, face-off angles, or smartphone usage during exams.
  • Randomized Question Shuffling: Dynamically generating randomized question order and randomized option positions per candidate to eliminate peer-to-peer copying.
  • Subjective Evaluation via LLM: Implementing automated NLP evaluation for short descriptive answers against faculty grading rubrics.
  • Mobile Apps & Offline Caching: Building dedicated React Native mobile apps with Progressive Web App (PWA) offline test caching in case of local network drops.
